
<h1> Observability </h1>

In Site Reliability Engineering (SRE), observability is the ability to understand what is happening inside a complex software system by looking at its outputs. If monitoring tells you that your house is on fire, observability helps you find the exact room, the faulty wire, and why it caught fire.

<h1> Monitoring vs. Observability </h1>
Monitoring: Tells you if a system is working or broken. It tracks known problems like high CPU usage or a website going down.
Observability: Tells you why it is broken. It lets you ask new, unexpected questions about system behavior you did not plan for.

<h2>The Three Pillars of Observability </h2>

Observability relies on three main types of data, often called the "three pillars": 

Metrics: Numerical data points that measure overall health (e.g., "CPU usage is at 80%").
Logs: Written event records (e.g., "Error: Database connection failed at 4:15 PM").
Traces: The full journey of a user request as it moves across different servers and microservices. 


<h2>Why It Matters for SREs</h2>
Faster Fixes: Helps engineers find the root cause of bugs quickly.
Less Downtime: Reduces the time it takes to recover from an outage.
Better Clarity: Makes hidden or unpredictable system interactions visible.
